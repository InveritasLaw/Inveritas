The First 48 Hours After a Charge

What happens in the next two days can shape the entire case. This is the plain-English checklist — what to do, and what you must never do, while everything is still in motion.

1

Stay silent — and say so out loud

You have the right to remain silent and the right to an attorney. Politely state that you are invoking both, then stop talking. Don't try to explain, argue, or "clear it up." Anything you say becomes part of the State's case.

2

Write down everything while it's fresh

Memory fades fast and gets less reliable under stress. Today, write a detailed timeline: who was there, what was said, what time things happened, what you saw. Date it. This becomes the backbone of your defense.

3

Preserve every piece of evidence

Photos, texts, call logs, receipts, location data, names and numbers of witnesses. Back it up somewhere safe. Don't delete anything — even things you think look bad. Deleting can become its own problem.

4

Do not contact the other party

If there's an alleged victim or witness — especially in a family or assault matter — do not call, text, or message them, directly or through someone else. It can violate a condition of release and create a brand-new charge.

5

Go dark on social media

Don't post about the case, the people involved, or your weekend. Prosecutors read it. Lock your accounts, and assume anything public will be used.

6

Capture every date and deadline

Court dates, filing windows, conditions of release. Missing a date can mean a warrant. Missing a filing deadline can mean waiving a defense you'll never get back.

7

Understand the charge before you decide anything

Before you accept any plea or strategy, understand exactly what the prosecution must prove — and where their case is weak. The leverage is written into the statute itself.
